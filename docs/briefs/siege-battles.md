## Build
1. **War parties**: on a siege/raid start (`SimEvent siege start`, and faction raid resolutions) spawn a visible war party sized from the siege strength (about 6-18 units) for the attacker kind: Blight (Hollows/hounds/brutes, `monster` entities using existing species) or a faction/bandit raid (human raiders in the attacker faction's colours: use `kind:'npc'`/'monster' as appropriate with `faction`, a `raider` role, looks/gear from role tables, a banner bearer and a captain/elite). They appear ~25-35 tiles from the target settlement on a passable spot along the connecting road (or the Blight source direction), then **march along the road to the town gate** (use `ctx.movement`, staggered speeds, formation-ish spacing), with a visible march cue (running animation if the renderer supports it).
2. **Defenders**: guards (and masters/legends in the settlement, plus militia: a handful of armed villagers promoted for the battle, at most ~8) leave their posts, run to the gate/approach and **fight** the attackers. Others hide indoors (existing npclife behaviour), shopkeepers close. Keep it **bounded**: only run the full battle simulation (positions, pairings, hits) while at least one online player is within AOI distance of the settlement; otherwise resolve instantly using the sim's outcome and leave no entities.
3. **Skirmish simulation (cheap, deterministic, no real combat engine for NPC vs NPC)**: units pair up with a nearest opponent, face each other, set `state:'fight'` and a facing, and exchange abstract hits on a timer. Hits produce `cfx` combat events (floating numbers/hit sparks via the existing `CombatEvent` hit/miss format) so spectators see them; HP bars from `hp/maxHp`; casualties play the death animation (`state:'dead'`) then are removed after a few seconds. Reinforcements and retreating survivors are fine. Strength/HP numbers derive from the sim's defense vs strength so the **visual outcome matches the sim result**: the sim decides (or you read its pending outcome) whether the town holds or falls; steer the battle: if it holds, attackers die or rout (run away and despawn) by the siege end; if it falls, defenders are overwhelmed, survivors flee, raiders reach the plaza, loot/celebrate (act cues), and fire/smoke appears on buildings (add a "burning" visual for fallen/besieged settlements if the renderer lacks it).
4. **Players take part**: players may attack raiders with the normal combat system (they are normal hostile monsters/NPCs in combat terms: reuse `monsterKilled` with `siege` set so defend missions, loot, XP and `sim.recordKill` work) and their kills count towards the defense (tilt the pending outcome in the town's favour per kill, capped; a heroic defense by several players should be able to save a town that would have fallen). Defenders and raiders can also fight players. Players standing inside a settlement during a siege should still be safe from *random* aggro as per existing rules unless they engage; raiders that reach the plaza may attack players.
5. **Atmosphere**: alarm cue (chronicle line, a `note` to nearby players "Raiders at the gates of X!"), `act` cues for defenders (`guard`/`patrol` shout-stance) and for villagers (`shelter`), drums/bell can be represented as a client-side visual pulse; after a victory NPCs `celebrate`, after a fall they `mourn`. Make sure the siege **ends cleanly**: all temporary entities removed, guards return to routines, settlement views updated, `sim` informed through its existing hooks (no duplicate effects: recordMissionDone applies its own world effects).
6. **Reconcile with the monsters module**: today it spawns siege waves outside gates; make exactly one module responsible so you don't double-spawn. On restart mid-siege, reconstruct from `sim.state.settlements[i].siegeUntil` (spawn a reduced party or resolve instantly).
